On an iPhone

There is no iPhone app, and there cannot be one. Here is the thing that does work instead — about two minutes to set up.

Why there is no app

iOS gives apps no way to send a text message on their own. The only messaging interface Apple exposes to a third-party app opens the Messages compose sheet with the text already filled in, and then waits for a person to press send. There is no entitlement that lifts this, no developer programme tier that unlocks it, and no sideloading trick around it. An App Store app, a TestFlight build and a self-signed app are all blocked identically.

So on iPhone the answer is not an app at all. It is a Shortcuts automation, which is an Apple feature and therefore allowed to do what apps are not.

Build it

  1. Open Shortcuts and start an automation

    Go to the Automation tab, then tap + in the top right.

  2. Choose Time of Day

    Pick a time when the phone is reliably on, awake and in signal. Mid-morning beats 3am, because a failure at 3am is one you will sleep through.

  3. Set it to repeat Monthly

    Pick a day of the month. Monthly is comfortably inside every carrier's inactivity window.

  4. Turn off “Ask Before Running”

    Confirm Run Immediately when it asks. Leave Notify When Run switched on — you want visible proof each month that it fired.

  5. Add the Send Message action

    Tap Next, then New Blank Automation, then add Send Message. Set the message to something short, and the recipient to another phone you own.

  6. Check it is sending by Messages

    Not WhatsApp. If the share sheet offers a choice, pick Messages. Then tap Done.

The blue bubble trap — this defeats most people.

If you text another iPhone, Messages sends it as an iMessage: over data, in a blue bubble. An iMessage is not a chargeable SMS and will not keep your SIM alive. Everything will look like it worked. It did not.

Force a real green-bubble SMS by doing one of these:

  • Text an Android phone. Simplest and most reliable.
  • Text a landline. It goes out as an SMS regardless of what happens at the other end.
  • Turn iMessage off entirely at Settings → Apps → Messages, if this iPhone is a spare whose only job is holding the number.

Before trusting any of this, send one by hand and look at the bubble. Green is SMS and counts. Blue is iMessage and does not.

Prove it actually runs

Do not wait a month to find out. Set the automation to a time three minutes away, put the phone down, and watch. Once you have seen it fire by itself and land as a green message on the other phone, edit it back to monthly.

Repeat that check after any major iOS update. Apple has changed the rules about automations running while the phone is locked more than once, and an automation that worked perfectly on one version can quietly start waiting for a tap on the next. That is the real weakness of this route, and it is worth knowing about rather than discovering in six months.

Give yourself a backstop

Add a repeating Reminder a fortnight before your carrier's actual cut-off that says “check the keep-alive text went out”. When it fires, open Messages and look at the last green message to that number. If it is stale, send one by hand. Thirty seconds, twice a year, and it covers every way the automation can silently stop.

Check your carrier

Inactivity windows vary and change — usually somewhere between 90 and 180 days. Search for your carrier's name plus “pay as you go inactivity disconnection”. Some carriers also require a top-up every so often regardless of activity, which no amount of texting will satisfy, so check for both rules.

The honest recommendation

If this number genuinely matters, the iPhone route is the weaker of the two. The Android app confirms with the network that each text actually left the handset, and tells you when one did not. iOS gives you no such confirmation — the automation reports that it ran, not that a chargeable message was delivered.

A cheap Android handset left on a charger with the SIM in it is far more dependable, and the app on it is free.
